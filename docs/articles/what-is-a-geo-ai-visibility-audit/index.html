What is a GEO or AI visibility audit?

Published 2026-10-30 by Keel AI Visibility. About 6 minutes.

"GEO" usually stands for generative engine optimisation: working to make a brand appear in the answers that AI assistants write. An AI visibility audit is the measurement that comes before any of that work. It answers a narrow, practical question: when people ask an AI assistant the questions that lead to your category, how often does the answer mention you, and which sources does it lean on?

This page defines the term, lists what a worthwhile audit contains and, just as usefully, what it cannot tell you.

What an AI visibility audit measures

At its core, an audit measures a mention rate: the share of AI answers, for a defined set of questions, that mention a brand. The word "defined" is doing the work. A good audit fixes in advance:

  • The assistant and settings. For example, the ChatGPT API with web search and a US location. Different assistants, models and locations give different answers.
  • The question themes. Typically six to fifteen, each reflecting something a buyer asks before choosing.
  • The wordings. Each theme asked several ways, because wording changes results.
  • The brands. Yours and a few named competitors, with agreed aliases and domains.
  • The detection rule. How a mention is recognised in the text.

Without those, "we are visible in AI" is an impression, not a finding.

What a useful report contains

  1. Mention rates with confidence intervals. A rate on its own, such as "we appear in 40% of answers", hides how uncertain it is. A 95% interval shows the range the true rate probably sits in.
  2. A competitor comparison on the same questions. Visibility is relative.
  3. Cited pages. Which pages on your site the answers linked to. This shows what the assistant can find and use.
  4. Cited third-party domains. The review sites, publishers and comparison pages that appear in answers. These are the places where coverage may matter.
  5. Wording sensitivity. Which themes swing when the question is rephrased. A theme where your rate runs from 60% to 100% depending on wording is fragile.
  6. Method and limits. Which model, what date, how sampling stopped, what the audit misses.

A fuller audit adds recommended actions, ranked by what the data actually supports.

An example of the shape of the findings

In an independent study of Teamwork.com in October 2026 (280 ChatGPT API answers, six themes), the result was not "good" or "bad". It was specific: the brand was mentioned in 90% to 98% of answers on most themes, but on resource planning it was mentioned in 25% of answers against 85% for Asana. That is a finding you can act on, because it points to one theme, one competitor and a list of cited sources. See the case study for the full table.

How it differs from an SEO audit

A traditional SEO audit looks at your site: crawlability, speed, content, links, and rankings for keywords. Search rankings are fairly stable and visible. AI answers are different in three ways that matter for measurement:

  • They vary. The same question can give different answers on different runs, so one observation is not a measurement. More on this.
  • There is no ranked list to read. A brand is either named in the answer or not, and its position is fuzzy.
  • The sources are partly off-site. Answers draw on third-party pages. An audit of your own site alone misses that.

The two are complementary. Strong SEO fundamentals help pages be found, and an AI visibility audit shows whether that translates into being named.

What an audit cannot do

  • It cannot guarantee anything. Nobody controls what an AI model says. Anyone promising guaranteed mentions or rankings is selling something an audit cannot deliver.
  • It does not show what every user sees. API answers can differ from the consumer app, which may use personalisation and memory.
  • It does not measure traffic or sales. Visibility in answers is a separate question from clicks, and your analytics answer the second.
  • It is a snapshot. Models update and the web changes. A rate measured in October may move by spring.
  • It shows association, not cause. If answers keep citing a review site and you are absent from it, that is a lead to investigate, not proof.

A note on terminology

The vocabulary is unsettled. "GEO", "AEO" (answer engine optimisation), "LLM SEO" and "AI search optimisation" are used loosely and often interchangeably, and there is no standards body. What matters is not the label but whether the thing being sold is a measurement with stated settings or a promise. When you read an offer, look for four things: which assistant and which settings, how many answers, how mentions are detected, and whether uncertainty is shown. If those are missing, the label does not matter, because the numbers cannot be checked.

What happens after the audit

The audit ends with a list of evidence-backed priorities. Typically these fall into three groups. First, content: pages that should exist, or be made clearer, for the themes where the brand is weak. Second, coverage: third-party sites that the answers keep citing and where the brand is missing or poorly described. Third, monitoring: re-running the same questions later to see whether the rates moved, using the interval to decide whether a change is real. None of these is automatic and none carries a guaranteed result, which is why the report separates what was measured from what is recommended.

It is also worth deciding up front who owns the follow-up. An audit that is read once and filed is wasted money. Name the person who will act on the top two findings before the report arrives.

When an audit is worth doing

It is most useful when you are deciding where to spend effort: which content to write, which sites to seek coverage on, which competitor to benchmark against. It is least useful as a vanity number. If a mention rate would not change what you do, you do not need to measure it.

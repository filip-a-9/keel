Why agencies buy audits instead of building them

A credible audit takes more effort than it looks. For one client you need 6 to 15 question themes, each in several wordings, asked repeatedly through an API, with matching rules for the brand and its competitors, and enough answers to give intervals narrow enough to mean something. In the independent Teamwork.com study that was 280 answers for six themes. Building the tooling, writing it up and checking it for one client is a poor use of an account manager's week; buying it for £249 or £749 is a known cost that can be priced into a project.

What to sell your client

Resist the urge to sell "AI SEO". The honest offer is:

  • A baseline. A measured mention rate per question theme, against named competitors, with 95% intervals.
  • A map of sources. The pages and third-party domains that the answers cited.
  • A short list of priorities. The themes with weak rates and the actions that follow from the evidence.
  • A re-measure. A second audit after the work, so change can be shown rather than asserted.

The audit feeds work you already sell: content, digital PR, listings and reviews, technical SEO. It does not replace them, and nobody can guarantee a result from them.

What you can promise, and what you cannot

This is where agencies protect themselves. Safe statements:

  • "On these questions, in the ChatGPT API with web search, your brand was mentioned in X% of answers (interval A to B)."
  • "These are the domains the answers cited most."
  • "These themes are sensitive to wording."

Statements to avoid:

  • "This is what customers see in ChatGPT." The audit uses the API and Claude Code's web search, not the consumer app or claude.ai, and answers may differ for an individual user.
  • "We will get you into ChatGPT's answers." Nobody can promise that.
  • "Your AI visibility is 40%." A single figure hides the themes, the intervals and the date.

Put the limits in your own proposal. A client who understands the limits at the start is much easier to keep than one who finds them later. The method page lists them in plain language and can be quoted.

How to present the numbers to a client

  1. Lead with the pattern, not the average. In the Teamwork.com study the story was five strong themes and one weak one (resource planning at 25% against 85% for Asana).
  2. Show the intervals. A one-page explainer on why intervals exist saves arguments; see why one screenshot proves nothing.
  3. Explain wording sensitivity with an example. One theme in that study ran from 60% to 100% across wordings.
  4. Turn the cited domains into a task list for your PR or content team.
  5. Agree a date for the re-measure.

Questions your client will ask

"Is this the same as what I see when I ask ChatGPT?" Not exactly. The audit uses the API, which is repeatable, and says so. A single manual query is one draw; the audit is many. If the client wants a sanity check, a few manual queries in the app are reasonable alongside the report, as long as nobody treats them as the finding.

"Will this fix it?" No. The audit shows where the gaps are and what the answers cite. The fix is your content, PR and listings work, measured again afterwards.

"Why Claude as well?" Different assistants can name different brands. The Full audit covers both ChatGPT and Claude so a client does not assume one result holds for the other.

"How often?" The audit is a snapshot. A sensible rhythm is a baseline, then a re-measure after the work has had time to take effect. Monthly monitoring is not part of either tier.
